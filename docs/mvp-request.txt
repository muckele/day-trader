1. MVP SCOPE AND PRODUCT CONTRACT
============================================================

The target is Day Trader MVP v1: an OWNER-ONLY application connected to
one designated Alpaca PAPER account.

The owner must be able to:
- Sign in securely.
- Research supported stocks and ordinary, unleveraged ETFs.
- View explainable recommendations with data timestamps and sources.
- Submit and manage supported paper orders.
- See broker-confirmed orders, fills, positions, and account performance.
- Use the existing trade-plan and research-to-trade workflows.
- Configure RoboTrader with enforceable risk and spending limits.
- Enable server-side RoboTrader so it continues operating after logout
  and when the browser is closed.
- Receive actual email notifications for RoboTrader execution events.
- Inspect decisions, rejection reasons, errors, and an audit trail.
- Disable automation and use a reliable emergency stop.

For this release:
- Alpaca paper trading is the supported broker execution environment.
- Live trading must be blocked at the server and broker-write layers.
- Automated entries are limited to explicitly supported long-only stocks
  and ordinary ETFs during regular trading hours, without leverage.
- Do not expand into options, shorts, margin, leveraged/inverse ETFs,
  overnight trading, or additional brokers.
- Preserve existing advanced modules, but disable or clearly mark them
  outside the released scope. Do not leave accessible unsafe endpoints.
- Preserve existing local simulation features only when unmistakably
  separated from Alpaca paper trading.
- Fractional orders may remain supported only for verified combinations.
  Where automated protection is not adequately implemented and tested,
  disable fractional automation with a clear explanation rather than
  silently sending unprotected orders.
- Do not make profitability, hedge-fund-equivalence, or guaranteed-ROI
  claims. Correct operation does not establish a profitable strategy.

This is not a public multi-user launch. Do not implement subscriptions,
billing, public onboarding, or brokerage OAuth as scope expansion.
Document those as a separate future milestone.

Keep the existing React, Node/Express, MongoDB/Mongoose, and deployment
architecture unless a specific MVP blocker requires a targeted change.
Do not migrate frameworks, databases, or hosting providers for preference.

5. ONE RELIABLE ORDER LIFECYCLE
============================================================

Use the existing broker and persistence modules where possible.
Do not create another parallel trading engine.

Establish a clear authoritative lifecycle for Alpaca-backed orders across
manual trading, trade plans, research, and RoboTrader.

For each logical order:
1. Validate authorization, account identity, mode, instrument, and inputs.
2. Evaluate the applicable risk policy against fresh account state.
3. Persist an intent and stable client_order_id before broker submission.
4. Reserve applicable capacity atomically.
5. Submit through the guarded Alpaca adapter.
6. Persist broker acknowledgement.
7. Reconcile status and fills idempotently.
8. Update derived views and enqueue notifications.

Distinguish intent, submission, acknowledgement, partial fill, full fill,
cancellation, rejection, replacement, expiration, and uncertain outcomes.

An accepted order is not a filled trade.
Do not update positions or realized P&L from an assumed fill price.

Handle:
- Duplicate clicks and request retries.
- Broker timeout after the broker accepted an order.
- Restart between submission and local acknowledgement.
- Local persistence or audit failure after broker acceptance.
- Duplicate and out-of-order broker events.
- Partial fills and partial-fill-then-cancel.
- Replacement chains and nested protective orders.

An error while saving an accepted order or writing an audit event must not
misclassify the order as definitely rejected or trigger a new order.

Persist and reuse the same client_order_id for retries of the same intent.
Investigate uncertain submissions by client_order_id. Do not treat one
temporary lookup miss as proof that the broker never accepted the order.

Use broker account, environment, and stable execution identifiers in
uniqueness and reconciliation rules.

Ensure all necessary unique indexes exist and are usable before enabling
execution. Index-bootstrap failures must affect execution readiness.

Use suitable decimal handling for money, prices, and fractional quantities;
apply broker precision rules without floating-point overspending.

Separate local simulator records from Alpaca paper records in storage
queries, calculations, APIs, and UI. Never combine them into one misleading
portfolio or performance curve.

6. ENFORCE THE ACTUAL RISK AND SPENDING LIMITS
============================================================

Trace every displayed setting from UI input through persistence to the
ACTIVE worker and the final execution decision.

Specifically inspect:
- dailyLimit, weeklyLimit, monthlyLimit
- maxTradeAmount and maxPositionSize
- maxDailyLoss and maxTradesPerDay
- maxOpenPositions
- allowed and blocked symbols
- asset-class restrictions
- cooldowns, pause state, and failure circuit breakers
- the global kill switch
- manual-approval thresholds

Do not count a limit as implemented because the legacy engine enforces it
or because a model contains the field.

Define spending limits separately from loss limits:
- Specify the day/week/month timezone and boundary rules.
- Count actual entry expenditure plus appropriate pending reservations.
- Reserve capacity before broker writes to prevent concurrent overspending.
- Reconcile reservations against fills and confirmed terminal states.
- Define conservative handling of replacements and uncertain orders.
- Do not replenish spending capacity simply because a position was sold.
- Define zero, null, missing, and invalid values explicitly and safely.

Use cash-aware sizing for this non-leveraged MVP rather than interpreting
all margin buying power as spendable cash.

Unknown account equity, P&L, tradability, market clock, or stale data must
not be silently converted into favorable values that approve entries.

Separate new-risk restrictions from risk-reducing exits. A reached loss
limit, trade-count limit, or cooldown must not automatically prevent a
valid protective exit. Account restrictions and order validity still apply.

Ensure all applicable paths share the core safety rules. Explain any
intentional manual-versus-automated policy differences.

7. RELIABLE BACKGROUND EXECUTION AND EMERGENCY STOP
============================================================

RoboTrader must run on the backend independently of browser activity,
session expiration, page polling, and user login state.

Keep only one canonical automated-entry engine enabled in the MVP.
Legacy routes must not bypass its controls or accidentally launch a
second independent trading loop.

Verify durable concurrency protection:
- Account/environment-scoped locking.
- Unique lock ownership.
- Lease renewal and ownership checks.
- Safe recovery after process death.
- No continued submissions after a worker loses its lease.
- Revalidation before broker submission.

Fix any enable/disable race.

In particular, inspect the worker's final RoboSettings.updateOne call:
an older settings snapshot must not overwrite a newer disable, emergency
stop, pause, mode change, or configuration update.

Treat user control state separately from worker telemetry.
A worker may update last-run information; it must not accidentally re-enable
itself.

Re-read or atomically validate current control state at the submission
boundary. Test disable/emergency-stop during an in-flight research run.

Emergency stop must:
- Persistently block new automated exposure.
- Survive reloads and backend restarts.
- Cancel eligible app-owned entry/increase orders safely.
- Preserve protective exits for existing positions.
- Avoid touching unrelated broker orders.
- Report already-in-flight requests and uncertain cancellation outcomes.
- Require explicit owner action to resume.

Account for broker-linked order groups: canceling one leg or a parent may
affect other protection. Do not blindly cancel every open order.

Flattening positions must be a separate, explicit, confirmed owner action,
not an undocumented consequence of stopping automation.

Reconciliation and necessary protection maintenance must remain functional
when new entries are disabled. A failing entry loop must not starve them.

On shutdown, stop scheduling new work and handle in-flight work safely.
